# Semantic Foundations

Truth, fact, knowledge, belief, data, confidence, accuracy, support, acceptance, inquiry, and value are routinely compressed into a single epistemic hierarchy. This compression can allow consensus, authority, institutional acceptance, confidence, belief, approval, or persistence to function as substitutes for represented support.

Aperta Veritas separates these terms before applying Recursive Truth Exposure.

## Veritas, truth, basis, and support

**Veritas** is the Latin word for truth.

Aperta Veritas does not define truth as whatever conclusion a system presently selects. RTE identifies conclusions presently supported as true under represented observations, relations, methods, measurements, conditions, and support claims. It does not establish that a conclusion is identical with definitive truth.

A **basis** is a represented reason, source, condition, relation, measurement, belief, rule, authority, criterion, or other represented element associated with acceptance, evaluation, inquiry, or support.

A basis does not become epistemic support merely because it contributes to a conclusion being accepted.

Authority, habit, loyalty, fear, reward, repetition, social consensus, policy, faith, or error can contribute to acceptance without thereby supporting the truth of the accepted conclusion.

An **acceptance basis** represents why an agent or system accepts, selects, retains, or acts upon a conclusion.

A **support relation** represents a claimed relation between one or more bases and whether a conclusion should presently be treated as true under represented conditions.

A **support claim** asserts that such a relation bears on whether the conclusion is true.

Support claims can involve observations, data, measurements, tests, predictions, contradictions, logical relations, provenance, explanatory relations, reproducibility, consequences, independent routes, and other represented relations.

The existence of a support claim does not certify that the claimed relation actually supports truth. The relation, its basis, inference rules, models, distinctions, methods, criteria, conditions, dependencies, exclusions, and alternatives remain open to Recursive Truth Exposure.

RTE can therefore ask separately:

```text
Why was this conclusion accepted?

What basis was cited?

What relation is claimed between that basis and the conclusion?

Why is that relation treated as relevant to whether the conclusion is true?

Under what conditions does that relation hold?

What observations, alternatives, contradictions, or dependencies could alter it?
```

Support is represented and conditioned. It is not identical to basis, confidence, consensus, authority, acceptance, or measurement.

A claim that one conclusion has greater support than another requires an explicit basis of comparison. The alternatives compared, distinctions and criteria used, relevant methods and conditions, support claims, and represented relations producing the comparison remain represented.

Where a comparative support claim depends on measurement, the distinctions, operationalization, measurements, methods, and conditions producing that comparison remain represented.

Where no represented comparison basis supports ranking, Aperta Veritas preserves the alternatives without ranking them.

Failure to represent a comparison basis does not establish that the alternatives are intrinsically incommensurable. They are only presently unranked under the represented framework. A claim of incommensurability is itself a conclusion requiring represented support.

A conclusion can have greater represented support than another without becoming 100 percent complete, absolutely accurate, or immune from revision.

Richer representation is not automatically greater accuracy. Added relations can alter support only through represented support claims whose relation to the conclusion remains examinable. Where an accuracy comparison depends on measurement, the relevant measurement remains explicit. Additional representation alone does not establish greater accuracy.

The distinction between a conclusion, the bases associated with it, and the support claims concerning it must itself remain represented.

## Inquiry basis

An **inquiry basis** represents why a claim, hypothesis, observation, anomaly, relation, distinction, alternative, or unresolved branch remains a candidate for further examination.

Inquiry basis is not identical to epistemic support.

A proposition can have weak or absent present support while retaining an inquiry basis. Unresolved observations, unexplained discrepancies, conflicting measurements, missing distinctions, incomplete models, untested predictions, unexplored alternatives, or the possibility of a discriminating test can justify continued examination without establishing the proposition as true.

Likewise, strong present support for a conclusion does not establish that alternative inquiry should permanently stop.

Aperta Veritas therefore preserves:

```text
failure to establish != disproof
failure to establish != basis for elimination
current support != future inquiry potential
```

A hypothesis does not need to be treated as true in order to remain represented and examinable.

Failure to produce present support is an epistemic result. It is not by itself a stopping rule.

Inquiry can generate new distinctions, relations, hypotheses, operationalizations, observations, tests, measurements, comparison bases, and support claims. These can make previously unresolved alternatives newly testable or comparable.

The inquiry basis itself remains open to Recursive Truth Exposure. Continued inquiry can consume resources, inherit values, privilege particular search spaces, or preserve unproductive branches. RTE therefore represents why inquiry continues, what resources it consumes, what alternatives receive or do not receive attention, and what stopping or reopening conditions are applied.

## Accuracy

Accuracy describes correspondence with what is true.

RTE does not possess an external position from which absolute correspondence can be certified. It can compare represented relations among conclusions, observations, predictions, consequences, contradictions, independent routes, measurements, support claims, and other specified conditions.

An **accuracy claim** therefore remains a claim. Its basis, comparison set, relevant measurements, methods, conditions, support claims, and unresolved alternatives remain available for examination.

Measured accuracy must not be silently compressed into definitive truth.

## Factum

**Factum** names a representation asserted or treated as fact.

A fact is a bounded conclusion treated as established under specified or unstated conditions.

Ordinary factual language can efficiently identify conclusions that have survived repeated observation and testing. The label does not establish that a conclusion is identical with definitive truth or close it against relevant revision.

Factum becomes epistemically consequential when the label compresses away the conditions under which the conclusion was established, represents bounded support as absolute certainty, or prevents renewed examination.

The statement "this is a fact" remains a claim with provenance, acceptance basis, support claims, conditions, and unresolved residuals.

> **Truth, fact, belief, acceptance, and inquiry are distinct. RTE examines represented support claims concerning conclusions treated as true. Factum identifies a conclusion treated as established. Neither operation certifies possession of definitive truth.**

## Datum, knowledge, claim, and conclusion

A **datum** is a retained representation produced by observation.

**Knowledge** is retained data, relations, methods, and conclusions available to an agent or system. Knowledge can contain observations, contradictions, transformations, fabrications, inactive branches, beliefs, and unresolved alternatives. Calling something knowledge identifies its availability. It does not certify its accuracy.

A **claim** is a representation presented as accurate or true under stated or unstated conditions.

A **conclusion** is a current synthesis of represented data, relations, inferences, methods, measurements, support claims, and conditions. Conclusions can be compared, restricted, divided, merged, superseded, reactivated, or preserved without ranking.

A conclusion is not identical with the basis for accepting it or the support claims concerning it. RTE preserves these separately.

## Fides and Pistis

**Fides** is Latin for belief, faith, or trust. **Pistis** is the Greek counterpart.

Belief does not become false merely because it exceeds what present support establishes, nor does belief establish its own truth.

Belief can contribute to acceptance, conflict with conclusions, anticipate later support, motivate inquiry, or remain unrelated to conclusions presently supported as true. Sincerity, confidence, loyalty, repetition, authority, and consensus can alter the persistence or distribution of belief without establishing the truth of its object.

### Rational faith

Rational faith preserves its basis, uncertainty, and conditions for revision.

A person or system can believe, trust, or act upon a proposition while explicitly representing that present support does not establish it as true.

Rational faith can therefore participate in open inquiry. Relevant observations, consequences, contradictions, measurements, support claims, and other represented relations remain capable of changing the belief or the confidence assigned to it.

### Blind faith

Blind faith insulates a belief from relevant distinction or suppresses the discrepancy between its represented support and the confidence assigned to it.

Blind faith is not defined by whether the proposition ultimately proves true or false. A blindly held belief can be true, and a rationally held belief can be false.

The distinction concerns the belief's relation to inquiry and revision.

Fides and Veritas therefore need not function as mutually exclusive categories. RTE preserves belief as belief rather than promoting it to truth or discarding it because it is not established.

Testing can produce new observations, distinctions, measurements, contradictions, consequences, and other represented relations. These can alter support claims and can rationally alter belief without producing a final conclusion.

## Confidence

Confidence represents the degree of certainty assigned to a claim, belief, or conclusion by an agent or system.

Confidence is not accuracy.

A highly confident conclusion can be poorly supported. A weakly held conclusion can be strongly supported. Changes in confidence are therefore represented separately from changes in support.

When confidence changes, RTE asks what observation, measurement, inference, belief, value, authority, reward, acceptance basis, support claim, or other relation produced the change.

## Value

A **value judgment** represents worth, preference, obligation, permission, prohibition, purpose, priority, rejection, protection, or a desired state relative to an evaluator.

Truth is not established by value.

Calling a conclusion desirable does not establish that it is true, and calling it undesirable does not establish that it is false. A value report can itself be accurately represented when it preserves the evaluator, criterion, conditions, and judgment rather than compressing them into an intrinsic property of the object.

Distinction, measurement, inquiry, comparison, and resource allocation also contain selections. A system selects what to distinguish, what to observe, which alternatives to compare, how distinctions are operationalized, which unresolved branches receive further examination, how resources are allocated, and when to stop.

RTE represents those selections and their operative values without reducing acceptance, inquiry, or represented support to value.

## Distinction, measurement, and relevance

A **distinction** specifies a variable, category, relation, boundary, or other basis by which possibilities can differ.

A distinction defines what can be differentiated. It may specify degrees, distributions, boundaries, changes, simultaneous relations, or unresolved ranges. It need not be binary.

A **measurement** is a represented result produced relative to one or more distinctions.

A measurement therefore depends on the distinctions applied, how they are operationalized, the object or process examined, the method of application, and the conditions under which the result is produced.

Measurements may be quantitative, qualitative, categorical, relational, comparative, bounded, or unresolved. Different distinctions can produce different measurements of the same object without those measurements necessarily conflicting.

A measurement does not become support merely because it exists. Its relation to a conclusion must remain represented separately.

A distinction is relevant to a conclusion when applying it could produce or alter a represented measurement, support claim, comparison, or other relation involved in that conclusion, including observational fit, prediction, explanatory coverage, contradiction, reproducibility, genealogy, conditions, or uncertainty.

A distinction can also be relevant to inquiry when applying it could expose a previously unavailable relation, hypothesis, test, comparison, or unresolved alternative.

Relevance is therefore itself a represented relation. It is not established merely because an evaluator declares information relevant or irrelevant.

Claims of relevance remain open to Recursive Truth Exposure.

## Semantic invariants

Aperta Veritas preserves the following distinctions:

```text
distinction != measurement
basis != support
acceptance_basis != support
inquiry_basis != support
support_claim != truth
conclusion != support
conclusion != acceptance_basis
confidence != support
confidence != accuracy
consensus != support
belief != truth
fact != definitive truth
value != truth
selection != improvement
relational richness != accuracy
best_supported != definitive_truth
failure_to_establish != disproof
failure_to_establish != elimination
current_support != future_inquiry_potential
unranked != necessarily_incommensurable
stopping != closure
inactive != erased
```

These distinctions are not protected from examination. They remain represented claims inside Aperta Veritas and are themselves subject to Recursive Truth Exposure.

## Recursive application

These definitions are not exempt from the process they define.

RTE can be applied to:

- truth;
- support;
- basis;
- acceptance;
- inquiry basis;
- support claims;
- accuracy;
- fact;
- belief;
- confidence;
- distinction;
- measurement;
- comparison;
- relevance;
- value;
- openness;
- closure;
- RTE itself.

If a later distinction exposes compression, contradiction, omitted alternatives, hidden criteria, or an unsupported relation in these definitions, the definitions can be revised while their earlier forms remain represented in the genealogy.

Aperta Veritas therefore does not require its present semantic foundations to be final in order to use them.

It requires them to remain open.
