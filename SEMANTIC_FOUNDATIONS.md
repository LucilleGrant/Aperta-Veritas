# Semantic Foundations

Truth, fact, knowledge, belief, data, confidence, accuracy, and value are routinely compressed into a single epistemic hierarchy. This compression can allow consensus, authority, institutional acceptance, confidence, belief, or approval to function as substitutes for represented support.

Aperta Veritas separates these terms before applying Recursive Truth Exposure.

## Veritas, truth, and support

**Veritas** is the Latin word for truth.

Aperta Veritas does not define truth as whatever conclusion a system presently selects. RTE identifies conclusions presently supported as true under represented observations, relations, methods, measurements, and conditions. It does not establish that a conclusion is identical with definitive truth.

Support is comparative and measured. Conclusions can be compared through represented dimensions such as observational fit, prediction, explanatory coverage, contradiction, reproducibility, or robustness under changed conditions. No one dimension establishes truth by category alone. The measurements, conditions, comparison set, methods, and unresolved alternatives remain represented.

A conclusion can have greater measured support than another without becoming 100 percent complete, absolutely accurate, or immune from revision.

Richer representation is not automatically greater accuracy. Added relations support a conclusion only when they alter an explicit measurement. When no shared measurement supports comparison, Aperta Veritas preserves the alternatives without ranking them.

The distinction between a conclusion and the represented support for treating it as true must itself remain represented.

## Accuracy

Accuracy describes correspondence with what is true.

RTE does not possess an external position from which absolute correspondence can be certified. It can measure represented relations between conclusions, observations, predictions, consequences, contradictions, independent routes, and other specified conditions.

An **accuracy claim** therefore remains a claim. Its basis, measurement, comparison set, conditions, and unresolved alternatives remain available for examination.

Measured accuracy must not be silently compressed into definitive truth.

## Factum

**Factum** names a representation asserted or treated as fact.

A fact is a bounded conclusion treated as established under specified or unstated conditions.

Ordinary factual language can efficiently identify conclusions that have survived repeated observation and testing. The label does not establish that a conclusion is identical with definitive truth or close it against relevant revision.

Factum becomes epistemically consequential when the label compresses away the conditions under which the conclusion was established, represents bounded support as absolute certainty, or prevents renewed examination.

The statement "this is a fact" remains a claim with provenance, measurements, conditions, and unresolved residuals.

> **Truth, fact, and belief are distinct. RTE measures support for conclusions represented as true. Factum identifies a conclusion treated as established. Neither operation certifies possession of definitive truth.**

## Datum, knowledge, claim, and conclusion

A **datum** is a retained representation produced by observation.

**Knowledge** is retained data, relations, methods, and conclusions available to an agent or system. Knowledge can contain observations, contradictions, transformations, fabrications, inactive branches, beliefs, and unresolved alternatives. Calling something knowledge identifies its availability. It does not certify its accuracy.

A **claim** is a representation presented as accurate or true under stated or unstated conditions.

A **conclusion** is a current synthesis of represented data, relations, inferences, methods, measurements, and conditions. Conclusions can be compared, restricted, divided, merged, superseded, reactivated, or preserved as incomparable.

A conclusion is not identical with the support for that conclusion. RTE preserves both.

## Fides and Pistis

**Fides** is Latin for belief, faith, or trust. **Pistis** is the Greek counterpart.

Belief does not become false merely because it exceeds what present measurement establishes, nor does belief establish its own truth.

Belief can support, conflict with, anticipate, or remain unrelated to conclusions presently supported as true. Sincerity, confidence, loyalty, repetition, authority, and consensus can alter the persistence or distribution of belief without establishing the truth of its object.

### Rational faith

Rational faith preserves its basis, uncertainty, and conditions for revision.

A person or system can believe, trust, or act upon a proposition while explicitly representing that present measurement does not establish it as true.

Rational faith can therefore participate in open inquiry. Relevant observations, consequences, contradictions, and measurements remain capable of changing the belief or the confidence assigned to it.

### Blind faith

Blind faith insulates a belief from relevant distinction or suppresses the discrepancy between its represented support and the confidence assigned to it.

Blind faith is not defined by whether the proposition ultimately proves true or false. A blindly held belief can be true, and a rationally held belief can be false.

The distinction concerns the belief's relation to inquiry and revision.

Fides and Veritas therefore need not function as mutually exclusive categories. RTE preserves belief as belief rather than promoting it to truth or discarding it because it is not established.

Testing produces new observations, distinctions, measurements, and representations. These can alter the support for a conclusion and can rationally alter belief without producing a final conclusion.

## Confidence

Confidence represents the degree of certainty assigned to a claim, belief, or conclusion by an agent or system.

Confidence is not accuracy.

A highly confident conclusion can be poorly supported. A weakly held conclusion can be strongly supported. Changes in confidence are therefore represented separately from changes in measured support.

When confidence changes, RTE asks what observation, measurement, inference, belief, value, authority, reward, or other relation produced the change.

## Value

A **value judgment** represents worth, preference, obligation, permission, prohibition, purpose, priority, rejection, protection, or a desired state relative to an evaluator.

Truth is not established by value.

Calling a conclusion desirable does not establish that it is true, and calling it undesirable does not establish that it is false. A value report can itself be accurately represented when it preserves the evaluator, criterion, conditions, and judgment rather than compressing them into an intrinsic property of the object.

Measurements and inquiry also contain selections. A system selects what to observe, which alternatives to compare, which dimensions to measure, how to allocate resources, and when to stop.

RTE represents those selections and their operative values without reducing represented support to value.

## Distinction and relevance

A **distinction** is measurement of a spectrum of possibility that produces a represented difference or relation.

A distinction can represent a degree, distribution, boundary, change, simultaneous relations, or unresolved range. It need not be binary.

A distinction is relevant to a conclusion when incorporating it can alter a represented relation involved in that conclusion or its support, including observational fit, prediction, explanatory coverage, contradiction, reproducibility, genealogy, conditions, uncertainty, or another represented measurement.

Relevance is therefore itself a represented relation. It is not established merely because an evaluator declares information relevant or irrelevant.

Claims of relevance remain open to Recursive Truth Exposure.

## Openness and closure

**Aperta** means open or uncovered.

Openness does not require doubt for its own sake. It means that conclusions, beliefs, methods, measurements, definitions, evaluators, criteria, and stopping conditions remain available for renewed examination when relevant distinctions arise.

Operational termination is not necessarily epistemic closure. A finite system can stop computation, choose an action, publish a conclusion, or allocate resources while preserving the conditions under which renewed examination can occur.

Epistemic closure occurs when a conclusion or belief is insulated from relevant distinction or when the conditions permitting revision are suppressed.

Aperta therefore applies both to conclusions and to the processes that produce, support, compare, retain, revise, or close them.

## Recursive application

Aperta Veritas applies these semantics recursively.

Its definitions of truth, support, accuracy, belief, relevance, value, openness, and closure remain subject to the same examination applied to other representations.

RTE cannot establish that its presently best-supported conclusion is identical with definitive truth.

It cannot establish that all relevant relations have been represented.

It cannot certify itself complete, neutral, value free, or finally accurate from an external position.

These limits remain represented rather than treated as exceptions to the framework.

> **No conclusion is exempt from the process by which conclusions are examined and revised, including conclusions about that process itself.**
