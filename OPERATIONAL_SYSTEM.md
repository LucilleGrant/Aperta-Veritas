# Operational System

Aperta Veritas is a recursive process for exposing truth. Its governing operation is Recursive Truth Exposure. It receives observations, data, claims, beliefs, values, models, contradictions, and anomalies. It produces current conclusions connected to measurements, provenance, alternatives, residuals, branches, transformations, and genealogy.

## Veritas

Veritas is the Latin word for truth. In Aperta Veritas, truth is the most accurate conclusion currently available under represented observations, relations, methods, and conditions.

Accuracy is comparative and measured. Scores, labels, consensus, confidence levels, and terminal states can contribute represented evidence, but none establishes accuracy by category alone. Their production conditions, dimensions, weights, exclusions, and relations to alternative conclusions remain within inquiry.

No conclusion reaches 100 percent completeness or finality. New observations, relations, methods, measurements, or alternatives can change which conclusion is most accurate.

## State

An inquiry state contains:

- **object:** the event, relation, system, or question under inquiry;
- **observation:** represented contact with the object;
- **datum:** information retained from an observation, source, instrument, or prior representation;
- **provenance:** the origin and transformation history of each datum and representation;
- **distinction:** measurement of a spectrum of possibility that produces a represented difference or relation;
- **claim:** a representation presented as accurate under stated or unstated conditions;
- **conclusion:** a current synthesis of represented data, relations, inferences, methods, and conditions;
- **comparison set:** the represented conclusions or alternatives included in an accuracy comparison;
- **accuracy measurement:** a represented comparison among conclusions under specified dimensions, methods, and conditions;
- **belief:** a representation accepted as true by an agent or system;
- **value:** Fides or Pistis concerning worth, preference, obligation, permission, purpose, priority, protection, rejection, or a desired state;
- **evaluator:** the distinctions, measurements, values, and selection operation producing a judgment or transition;
- **model:** a connected set of representations used to describe, explain, predict, compare, or act;
- **boundary:** a limit on observation, testing, speech, revision, scope, or resource use;
- **game:** the evaluator, value labels, dependencies, resource conditions, selections, and exclusions shaping transitions in an agent or system;
- **test:** an operation that places a claim or model in contact with observation, contradiction, consequence, comparison, or a competing representation;
- **residual:** a represented limit, uncertainty, exclusion, incomplete operation, or unresolved branch outside the active conclusion but connected to its genealogy;
- **genealogy:** the preserved sequence of inputs, transformations, tests, branches, measurements, and revisions;
- **status:** a description of the current relation between a representation and completed tests.

Status descriptions record operations and relations: untested, tested under stated conditions, contradicted by a specified observation, scope-restricted, superseded, unresolved, incomparable, and reactivated. Status does not assign intrinsic worth, absolute accuracy, or finality.

## Operations

### 1. Encounter

Receive an observation, datum, claim, belief, contradiction, model, desire, or anomaly without converting its input category into an accuracy status.

### 2. Observe through distinction

Perform or register a distinction. Record the spectrum, difference, or relation measured and whether observation alters the observed relations.

### 3. Identify

Identify the observations, distinctions, data, claims, inferences, conclusions, provenance, context, relations, transformations, branches, value judgments, and residuals. Identify the observer, instrument, language, categories, scale, conditions, and measurement protocol.

### 4. Record

Record the identified material as an inquiry state. Connect the inquiry state to the source and prior states. Represent compression and selection as transformations.

### 5. Separate

Separate observation from datum, datum from claim, claim from conclusion, conclusion from belief, accuracy from confidence, knowledge from finality, measurement from value, and fact from closed truth.

### 6. Map the game

Record the declared account of selection and the observed transition. Identify the evaluator, measurement dimensions, value labels, audiences, dependencies, protected states, resource conditions, active states, inactive states, and exclusions.

### 7. Map boundaries

Identify where observation, representation, testing, comparison, revision, or permissible speech stops. Record what each boundary preserves, excludes, compresses, or conceals without assigning a function in advance.

### 8. Generate branches

Produce competing explanations, inversions, counterexamples, changes of scale, alternative distinctions, and unrepresented possibilities. Record branches even when current resources prevent full testing.

### 9. Test

Place each representation in contact with available observations, consequences, contradictions, independent routes, and competing models. Record the test conditions and the relations each test can and cannot examine.

### 10. Compare

Measure relations among conclusions. Recorded dimensions can include observational fit, prediction and observed outcome, explanatory coverage, contradiction, reproducibility through independent routes, robustness under changed conditions, preservation of previously supported relations, provenance continuity, scope, dependencies, compression loss, residuals, resource cost, and reversibility.

No comparison dimension determines accuracy alone. Any combined score remains a represented decision rule with recorded dimensions, weights, conditions, and exclusions.

Relational richness does not count as accuracy unless the added relations alter an explicit measurement. When no shared measurement supports comparison, preserve the conclusions as unresolved or incomparable rather than manufacturing a ranking.

### 11. Conclude

Identify the most accurate conclusion available within the represented comparison set and conditions. Record its supporting measurements, alternatives, limitations, and residuals.

The comparison set may omit an unrepresented conclusion that would outperform every represented alternative. Therefore, the current truth remains open.

### 12. Revise

Revise, restrict, split, merge, supersede, or reactivate representations and conclusions. Record the prior state and the reason for transition. Revision can change which conclusion is currently most accurate without making the new conclusion final.

### 13. Perform Recursive Truth Exposure

Apply every operation to the system, evaluator, accuracy measurements, evaluation criteria, comparison set, retained genealogy, current conclusion, allocation of attention, and decision to continue or stop.

Record proxy substitution whenever approval, confidence, consensus, coherence, novelty, preservation, efficiency, survival, or improvement governs selection while represented as accuracy.

### 14. Continue

Return the revised state as input. Inquiry stops locally because of a represented boundary such as time, evidence, risk, or resources. Local stopping does not convert the current conclusion into final truth.

## Transition record

Each transition records:

- prior state identifier;
- operation applied;
- inputs and provenance;
- distinctions introduced or removed;
- boundaries encountered;
- declared and operative games;
- values and evaluator state;
- observations and tests;
- comparison set and accuracy dimensions;
- competing branches;
- transformation performed;
- current conclusion and status;
- residuals and uncertainty;
- resource cost;
- conditions for reopening the transition.

## Output

An Aperta Veritas output contains:

- the most accurate currently available conclusion, when comparison supports one;
- the measurements and conditions supporting that designation;
- its scope and genealogy;
- active, inactive, unresolved, and incomparable alternatives;
- contradictory observations;
- unresolved residuals and known boundaries;
- the limits of the comparison set;
- conditions that can trigger revision.

The output does not announce final truth. It exposes why a conclusion is currently designated most accurate and keeps that designation available for renewed examination.

## Closure conditions

Closure is recorded when:

- a conclusion is declared absolutely accurate, complete, or final;
- confidence is treated as accuracy;
- consensus is treated as accuracy;
- knowledge is treated as necessarily accurate;
- belief is assigned accuracy or rationality by category;
- Factum ends revision;
- a rejected branch is erased rather than preserved with a rejection basis;
- provenance is removed;
- a boundary receives a function before testing;
- relational richness is treated as accuracy without measurement;
- an evaluation score replaces the represented dimensions, conditions, weights, and exclusions that produced it;
- the represented comparison set is treated as containing every possible conclusion;
- the process exempts its own terms, methods, author, outputs, evaluator, measurements, or continuation from examination.

## Minimum implementation

A minimal implementation can operate as a versioned ledger. Each node stores an inquiry state. Each edge stores a transition record. Branches preserve competing conclusions. Tests update relational statuses. Comparisons can designate the most accurate currently available conclusion without issuing a certificate of finality. Retrieval can reactivate prior branches when new evidence, distinctions, resources, methods, measurements, or contexts appear.

The implementation can be performed by a person, group, institution, or machine. The medium changes the resource constraints and observed closure conditions. The process remains Recursive Truth Exposure.
