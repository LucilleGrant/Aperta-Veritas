# Open Problems and Reviewer Guide

## Status

This solicits adversarial review. It does not assert that AV, RTE, or the proposed protocol has been validated.

## Highest-priority open problems

1. Is RTE operationally distinct from structured self-critique, debate, process supervision, or a larger fixed rubric?
2. What stopping rule prevents evaluator-of-evaluator expansion from becoming performative recursion?
3. Does exposure change reachable inquiry or merely relocate a fixed boundary into a larger prompt?
4. What distinguishes an unauthorized evaluator from a legitimate implicit constraint?
5. Can “support claim” be scored without importing the reviewer’s epistemology?
6. What distinguishes useful Open Genealogy from retained context or verbose logging?
7. Which finite compression/deletion policies preserve recontextualization potential?
8. Can allocation be represented without mistaking narrative traces for mechanisms?
9. Can relevance rubrics avoid candidate flooding and AV vocabulary advantage?
10. When is an RTE ledger evidence rather than post hoc rationalization?
11. How should human and model graders be combined when both inherit evaluator bias?
12. Do effects generalize across models, domains, languages, and adversarial authors?
13. Are any gains worth added tokens, latency, storage, and adjudication labor?
14. When should genealogy be redacted or deleted for privacy and safety?
15. Which negative result should narrow the architecture rather than trigger another recursion layer?

## Requested review

Reviewers are invited to provide a counterexample, nearest-prior-work comparison, protocol confound, failure task, more economical treatment, measurement repair, safety objection, or falsification criterion. Agreement or endorsement is not requested.

## Five potential targets

No contact has been made.

### 1. Jacob Steinhardt — UC Berkeley

**Fit:** His stated work covers reliable and value-aligned ML, robustness, reward specification, scalable alignment, and evaluation—directly relevant to evaluator dependence and misspecification.

**Ask:** Does the protocol distinguish evaluator exposure from ordinary robustness or reward misspecification, and are resource controls credible?

**Route:** [Berkeley Statistics profile](https://statistics.berkeley.edu/people/jacob-steinhardt) lists `jsteinhardt@berkeley.edu`.

### 2. Owain Evans — Truthful AI / Berkeley CHAI affiliate

**Fit:** His work includes TruthfulQA, LLM introspection, self-explanation, and argument evaluation, relevant to separating self-critique from faithful process exposure.

**Ask:** Are RTE traces post hoc self-explanations rather than evidence about process, and is the baseline fair?

**Route:** [Research page](https://owainevans.github.io/) links current work and public channels. `owaine@gmail.com` appears in the [TruthfulQA paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07958); verify before sending.

### 3. Finale Doshi-Velez — Harvard SEAS

**Fit:** Her research on interpretability, statistical validation, and decision-making under uncertainty bears directly on construct validity and explanation-as-validation risks.

**Ask:** Does the structured trace expose inspectable information beyond a length-matched control?

**Route:** [Harvard profile](https://kempnerinstitute.harvard.edu/people/our-people/finale-doshi-velez/) lists `finale@seas.harvard.edu`; her lab page lists `finale-lab@g.harvard.edu` for group meeting requests.

### 4. David Danks — University of Virginia

**Fit:** His work spans philosophy, cognitive science, ML, causal reasoning, and epistemic evaluation, matching the distinctions among authority, acceptance, and support.

**Ask:** Identify category errors and cases where an evaluator is legitimate despite lacking explicit authorization.

**Route:** [UVA profile](https://datascience.virginia.edu/people/david-danks) lists `eww3bh@virginia.edu`.

### 5. Melanie Mitchell — Santa Fe Institute

**Fit:** Her work on abstraction, analogy, reasoning, and principles for evaluating cognitive capabilities is relevant to missing distinctions, benchmark validity, and performance-versus-competence errors.

**Ask:** Does the benchmark measure transferable inquiry capability or compliance with RTE categories?

**Route:** [SFI profile](https://www.santafe.edu/people/profile/melanie-mitchell) lists `mm@santafe.edu`; her [personal site](https://melaniemitchell.me/) asks senders to read her email note first.

## Suggested order

Start with David Danks for conceptual distinctions, Finale Doshi-Velez for construct validity, Jacob Steinhardt for controls, Owain Evans for introspection confounds, and Melanie Mitchell for benchmark validity. This is an allocation recommendation, not a qualification ranking. Contact only after author approval.
