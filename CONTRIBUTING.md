# Contributing to Aperta Veritas

Aperta Veritas can receive criticism without converting criticism into erasure or requiring agreement as the price of participation.

## Contribution branches

- identify an unrepresented relation;
- expose a hidden dependency or incentive;
- provide a counterexample;
- restrict the scope of an overgeneralized claim;
- propose another representation and record the distinctions producing it;
- identify a compression loss;
- test a boundary;
- formalize an operation;
- construct an adversarial case;
- add provenance or reproduction conditions;
- demonstrate that the framework is calculating for a proxy rather than Veritas.

## Contribution format

Whenever practical, include:

1. the claim or operation being addressed;
2. the proposed change;
3. the observations, measurements, reasoning, or experience connected to it;
4. relevant scope and context;
5. known costs, exclusions, and uncertainty;
6. whether the change contradicts a specified observation, restricts scope, splits, supersedes, or reactivates an existing representation.

## Anti-closure rule

Agreement with the author and hostility toward the framework are represented relations, not truth criteria. Contributions enter inquiry with their relations, consequences, provenance, boundaries, and genealogy recorded.

## History

Substantial revisions preserve the genealogy of the claims being altered. A superseded position remains represented rather than being silently rewritten into a claim that the superseded position never existed.

## Conduct

Critique claims, distinctions, methods, and incentives directly. Harassment, threats, disclosure of private information, and attempts to prevent others from participating are not Boundary Testing; they obstruct the inquiry environment.
