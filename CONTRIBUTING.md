# Contributing to Aperta Veritas

Aperta Veritas is open to criticism, contradiction, revision, alternative representations, new distinctions, new generators, new allocators, and tests of its own assumptions.

Agreement with the framework is not a condition of contribution.

A contribution also does not gain support merely by opposing the framework.

Claims, counterclaims, distinctions, measurements, bases, acceptance bases, inquiry bases, inquiry operations, allocation bases, inquiry priorities, resource allocations, support claims, methods, generators, allocators, evaluators, comparison bases, boundaries, and revisions remain subject to the same Recursive Truth Exposure process.

- identifying an unrepresented relation;
- identifying a hidden dependency, assumption, incentive, generator, allocator, or evaluator;
- providing a counterexample;
- contradicting an existing claim with represented observations, measurements, reasoning, tests, or support claims;
- restricting the scope of an overgeneralized claim;
- proposing an alternative representation;
- identifying a compression loss;
- identifying a distinction that has been collapsed;
- identifying a distinction incorrectly treated as a measurement;
- identifying a basis incorrectly treated as epistemic support;
- identifying an acceptance basis incorrectly treated as support;
- identifying an inquiry basis incorrectly treated as support;
- identifying an inquiry basis incorrectly treated as inquiry priority;
- identifying inquiry priority incorrectly treated as support;
- identifying an allocation basis incorrectly treated as support;
- identifying resource allocation incorrectly treated as support;
- identifying support incorrectly reduced to measurement;
- identifying a support claim whose truth-relevance has not been established;
- testing a boundary;
- identifying a generative boundary;
- identifying an allocation boundary;
- formalizing an operation;
- constructing an adversarial case;
- identifying an unrepresented alternative;
- identifying a candidate-generation failure;
- identifying an inquiry-generation failure;
- identifying an allocation failure;
- identifying inquiry starvation;
- identifying a comparison-set exclusion;
- identifying an unstated comparison basis;
- adding provenance or reproduction conditions;
- identifying a proxy substituted for represented support;
- identifying confidence, consensus, value, selection, reward, priority, allocation, or belief substituted for truth or support;
- proposing a new distinction;
- challenging an existing distinction;
- proposing a new measurement;
- challenging an existing measurement;
- proposing or challenging a support relation;
- proposing or challenging a support claim;
- proposing or challenging an inquiry basis;
- proposing or challenging an allocation basis;
- proposing or challenging an inquiry priority;
- proposing or challenging a resource allocation;

- showing that a later distinction changes the represented relations among retained records;
- showing that changed allocation conditions make a previously inactive inquiry operationally accessible;
- showing that retained genealogy enables a candidate, hypothesis, relation, test, or inquiry operation that was previously unavailable.

Contributions do not need to preserve the current architecture if they expose a reason to revise it.

## Contribution format

Whenever practical, include:

1. the claim, representation, operation, implementation, generator, allocator, evaluator, or boundary being addressed;
2. the proposed change or competing representation;
3. the observations, measurements, tests, reasoning, implementation results, experience, bases, or support claims connected to it;
4. relevant distinctions and their operationalization where applicable;
5. relevant methods and conditions;
6. the acceptance basis where the contribution concerns why something is accepted, selected, retained, or acted upon;
7. the inquiry basis where the contribution concerns a represented basis under which further examination could occur;
8. the possible inquiry operation where the contribution concerns how further examination could proceed;
9. the allocation basis where the contribution concerns how finite resources are distributed;
10. the inquiry priority where possible inquiries are ordered or preferred;
11. the resource allocation and activation state where operational attention or resources are relevant;
12. the support relation and support claim where the contribution concerns whether something should presently be treated as true;
13. the comparison set and comparison basis where a comparative claim is being made;
14. relevant measurements where the comparison depends on measurement;
15. the generator where candidate or inquiry-operation production is relevant;
16. the allocator where prioritization or resource assignment is relevant;
17. the evaluator and criteria where evaluation, selection, or value judgment is involved;
18. relevant scope and context;
19. known dependencies, exclusions, resource conditions, and residual uncertainty;
20. what observation, test, distinction, measurement, contradiction, relation, support claim, allocation change, or other represented change could alter the proposed conclusion or inquiry state;
21. whether the contribution contradicts, restricts, splits, supersedes, reactivates, recontextualizes, or remains currently unranked relative to an existing representation.

Not every contribution requires every field.

The purpose is to preserve enough structure to distinguish:

```text
from

inquiry priority

from
allocation

from
epistemic support

from

support

```

A contribution can provide material for further inquiry without being treated as true.

A contribution can remain open to examination without receiving current priority or resources.

A contribution can also be accepted for implementation without its acceptance basis becoming epistemic support for every claim it contains.

## Comparative claims

Claims that one representation, method, generator, allocator, evaluator, implementation, or conclusion is more accurate, better supported, more efficient, or otherwise preferable should identify the basis of comparison and the conditions under which the comparison is made.

A comparison basis can include:

- the alternatives being compared;
- represented support relations and support claims;
- distinctions;
- criteria;
- methods;
- conditions;
- measurements where applicable;
- generator structure where applicable;
- allocator structure where applicable;
- evaluator structure where applicable;
- allocation history where relevant;
- dependencies;
- exclusions;
- unresolved relations.

For example:

```text
A has greater represented support than B
under comparison basis K
within comparison set S
under conditions C.
```

Where comparison basis `K` depends on measurement, the relevant distinctions, operationalization, measurements, methods, and conditions should remain represented.

A comparative claim does not require all support to be reduced to a common measurement.

If no represented basis supports comparison, preserve the alternatives without ranking them.

Represent this as:

```text
currently unranked
under represented comparison basis K
```

where appropriate.

Failure to represent a comparison basis does not establish that no comparison basis exists.

Do not classify alternatives as intrinsically incommensurable merely because the current framework cannot rank them.

A claim of incommensurability is itself a claim requiring represented support.

## Distinction and measurement

A distinction specifies what can differ.

A measurement is a represented result produced relative to one or more distinctions.

Contributions should not silently collapse these operations.

Where relevant, preserve:

```text
distinction
-> operationalization
-> observation or test
-> measurement or other represented result
```

This is not a mandatory linear sequence.

A contribution can challenge the distinction itself, its operationalization, the resulting measurement, or the support claim connecting that result to a conclusion.

A new distinction can also expose relations among prior records that were not representable when those records were produced.

Therefore a contribution can legitimately alter the current relational context of earlier records without rewriting those earlier records.

## Basis, inquiry, allocation, and support

A basis is a represented reason, condition, source, relation, measurement, belief, rule, authority, criterion, or other element associated with acceptance, evaluation, inquiry, allocation, or support.

A basis is not automatically epistemic support.

An acceptance basis records why an agent or system accepts, selects, retains, or acts upon a conclusion.

An inquiry basis is a represented basis under which further examination of a claim, hypothesis, observation, anomaly, relation, distinction, alternative, unresolved branch, or other represented object could occur.

An inquiry operation is a represented possible continuation of inquiry.

An allocation basis represents criteria, conditions, constraints, purposes, policies, values, costs, expected consequences, or other bases used in allocating resources among possible inquiry operations.

An inquiry priority is a represented ordering or preference among possible inquiry operations under an explicit allocation basis and represented conditions.

A resource allocation is a represented assignment of available resources to one or more inquiry operations under represented conditions.

An allocator is a represented process or agent participating in prioritization and resource allocation.

A support relation is a claimed relation between one or more bases and whether a conclusion should presently be treated as true under represented conditions.

A support claim asserts that such a relation bears on whether the conclusion is true.

Therefore:

```text
basis != support
acceptance_basis != support
inquiry_basis != support
inquiry_basis != inquiry_priority
inquiry_priority != support
allocation_basis != support
resource_allocation != support
inquiry_priority != resource_allocation
support_claim != truth
```

Authority, policy, consensus, reward, habit, repetition, inherited state, or system architecture can explain why something is accepted without thereby supporting its truth.

An anomaly, unresolved contradiction, missing distinction, incomplete model, unexplored alternative, or possible discriminating test can provide an inquiry basis without supporting a particular explanation as true.

An inquiry basis does not establish that an inquiry should receive present priority.

An inquiry priority does not establish that resources will be allocated.

A resource allocation does not establish epistemic support.

A measurement does not become support merely because it exists.

A represented support claim must specify how a basis is claimed to bear on the conclusion under examination.

Likewise, a support relation does not become a measurement merely because it participates in comparison.

Contributors should therefore preserve, where relevant:

```text
basis
-> claimed support relation
-> support claim concerning conclusion
```

and separately:

```text
inquiry basis
-> possible inquiry operation
-> allocation basis
-> inquiry priority
-> resource allocation
-> active inquiry
```

The second sequence is not mandatory or automatic.

An inquiry operation can remain represented without receiving priority or resources.

A high-priority inquiry can remain inactive when required resources or dependencies are unavailable.

The support claim, allocation basis, priority, and allocation decision each remain open to examination.

## Convergent Inquiry

**Convergent Inquiry** is the generation and examination of possible continuations of unresolved inquiry, including new distinctions, relations, hypotheses, operationalizations, methods, observations, tests, measurements, comparison bases, and support claims.

A contribution can therefore concern not only an answer but the generation of a new way to continue inquiry.

Possible contributions include:

- generating a distinction unavailable to the current inquiry;
- generating an alternative hypothesis;
- generating a discriminating test;
- generating a new operationalization;
- generating a new comparison basis;
- generating a new support claim;
- identifying a retained branch that can be reexamined;
- identifying a new relation among retained records;
- generating a new inquiry operation.

Convergent Inquiry does not establish that continued investigation supports a preferred conclusion.

It does not establish that one unresolved object should receive resources before another.

It does not require every inquiry basis to remain operationally active.

The term *convergent* does not assert mathematical convergence, monotonic improvement, a fixed limit, or guaranteed approach to truth.

A claim that an inquiry converges remains a claim requiring represented support.

Contributors should preserve:

```text
inquiry_generation != inquiry_priority
inquiry_generation != resource_allocation
```

A possible inquiry can remain represented without becoming active.

## Generation, allocation, and evaluation

Evaluation operates on represented candidates or results.

It does not by itself determine which candidates become represented.

Allocation participates in determining which represented possibilities receive finite resources and become active.

It does not by itself determine which possibilities become representable.

Contributors should preserve:

```text
generation != allocation
allocation != evaluation
generation != evaluation
```

A contribution concerning search, discovery, hypothesis generation, successor generation, test generation, inquiry-operation generation, or representation should identify the relevant generator where practical.

A generator can be constrained by:

- architecture;
- current distinctions;
- representation formats;
- training;
- search procedures;
- retrieval;
- tools;
- environmental access;
- allocator feedback;
- evaluator feedback;
- policy;
- compute;
- memory;
- retained genealogy;
- pruning;
- randomness.

A contribution concerning prioritization, activation, resource assignment, or operational attention should identify the relevant allocator where practical.

An allocator can be constrained or conditioned by:

- allocation bases;
- inquiry priorities;
- compute;
- memory;
- storage;
- energy;
- time;
- bandwidth;
- environmental access;
- tool availability;
- cost;
- tractability;
- urgency;
- novelty;
- anomaly density;
- expected consequences;
- policy;
- values;
- purposes;
- dependencies;
- generator feedback;
- evaluator feedback;
- randomness.

A candidate absent from evaluation may have been rejected before selection.

It may also never have been generated.

It may have been generated but never allocated sufficient resources to become active.

These are different events.

Therefore:

```text
evaluated_candidates != exhaustive_possibility_space
absence_from_search != disproof
not_allocated != rejected
```

A contribution can expose a generative failure without already possessing the missing solution.

A contribution can also expose an allocation failure without establishing that the inactive possibility would have produced a different conclusion.

For example, demonstrating that a candidate class cannot be represented under the current generator is itself relevant even when the contributor cannot yet generate the required candidate.

Likewise, demonstrating that a represented inquiry repeatedly receives insufficient resources can expose an allocation boundary even when the result of executing that inquiry remains unknown.

## Open and active inquiry

Open inquiry and active inquiry remain distinct.

Open inquiry permits relevant future examination or revision.

Active inquiry is currently receiving resources for examination.

Therefore:

```text
open_inquiry != active_inquiry
not_allocated != rejected
inactive != erased
```

A contribution can remain open while inactive.

A branch can possess an inquiry basis while receiving no current resources.

A previously inactive branch can become active after:

- new observations;
- new distinctions;
- new relations;
- new methods;
- new tools;
- new resources;
- allocator revision;
- changed allocation basis;
- changed inquiry priority;
- changed dependencies;
- generator revision;
- evaluator revision;
- failure of another branch.

Operational inactivity should not silently become epistemic closure.

Likewise, openness does not require unlimited computation or permanent activation.

Finite systems require allocation.

The relevant allocation relation should remain represented where practical.

## Criticism and contradiction

Criticism is not disproof by category.

Contradiction is not automatically decisive.

A criticism can expose:

- a false claim;
- an unsupported inference;
- an omitted alternative;
- an inadequate distinction;
- a defective operationalization;
- an invalid measurement;
- a basis incorrectly classified as support;
- a missing support relation;
- a defective support claim;
- an inquiry basis incorrectly treated as priority;
- an inquiry priority incorrectly treated as support;
- an allocation basis incorrectly treated as support;
- a resource allocation incorrectly treated as support;
- an inappropriate comparison basis;
- an implementation error;
- an unstated generator;
- an unstated allocator;
- an unstated evaluator;
- a generation failure;
- an allocation failure;
- inquiry starvation;
- an overgeneralization;
- a representation limit;
- a genuine unresolved contradiction.

The criticism and the original claim both remain represented strongly enough to examine the relation between them where practical.

A contradiction should identify what is contradicted and the represented basis of the contradiction.

A criticism can itself be unsupported, incorrectly framed, generated from an inadequate distinction, or privileged by an unexamined allocation basis.

It remains subject to the same inquiry.

## Alternatives and inactive branches

A competing representation should not be erased merely because another branch is selected or allocated resources.

When practical, preserve:

- the alternative;
- its acceptance basis where relevant;
- its inquiry basis;
- possible inquiry operations;
- its support claims;
- its contradictions;
- its relation to the selected branch;
- the comparison basis used;
- the allocation basis where relevant;
- its inquiry priority where represented;
- its resource allocation;
- its activation state;
- the selection event;
- the reason it became inactive;
- conditions under which it could become active or relevant again.

Inactive does not mean false.

Unallocated does not mean rejected.

Superseded does not mean erased.

Selection does not mean improvement.

Failure to establish does not mean disproof.

Failure to establish does not by itself provide a basis for elimination.

Current support does not determine all future inquiry potential.

A branch with weak current support can remain represented because a later distinction, observation, relation, method, test, allocation change, or resource change could make it relevant.

This does not require treating the branch as true or assigning resources to it now.

## Recontextualization

Retained records can participate in relations that were unavailable when those records were created.

For example:

```text
retained record A
+
retained record B
+
new distinction D
->
new represented relation R
```

The new relation does not rewrite the historical records.

It adds a later relational context.

A contribution can therefore recontextualize earlier material while preserving the genealogy of both the earlier representation and the later relation.

Where practical, record:

- the prior records;
- their earlier represented relations;
- the new distinction;
- the newly represented relation;
- the generator or operation that exposed it;
- any resulting hypothesis, test, measurement, comparison, candidate, or inquiry operation;
- the allocation conditions affecting whether the new inquiry became active;
- the support claims concerning the new relation;
- unresolved alternatives.

Newly generated relation does not mean established relation.

Recontextualization creates material for inquiry.

It does not certify its own accuracy.

Likewise, generating a possible inquiry from a new relation does not establish that resources should be allocated to it.

## Boundary Testing

A contribution can challenge a boundary.

A boundary can restrict:

- observation;
- representation;
- distinction;
- measurement;
- hypothesis generation;
- candidate generation;
- inquiry-operation generation;
- prioritization;
- resource allocation;
- activation;
- testing;
- comparison;
- evaluation;
- selection;
- revision;
- action;
- reopening.

Pressure against a boundary does not establish that the boundary is false, harmful, or unnecessary.

Resistance by the boundary does not establish that it is true, beneficial, or necessary.

Where a test crosses or pressures a boundary, record:

- the boundary;
- the test;
- the generator where relevant;
- the allocator where relevant;
- the allocation basis where relevant;
- inquiry priority where relevant;
- resource allocation and activation state where relevant;
- relevant distinctions;
- relevant observations or measurements;
- bases;
- support claims;
- conditions;
- what changed;
- what did not change;
- newly available or unavailable candidates;
- newly active or inactive inquiries;
- unresolved interpretations.

Claims about the function of the boundary remain claims requiring represented support.

A boundary can prevent generation of the distinction or test required to expose it.

A boundary can also permit a test to become representable while preventing allocation of the resources required to execute it.

Failure to cross a boundary therefore does not by itself establish that the boundary is justified.

## Values, priorities, allocators, and evaluators

Contributors can propose values, criteria, priorities, allocation procedures, allocator structures, and evaluator structures.

Their status as values, priorities, allocators, or evaluators does not establish or negate truth.

When a contribution calls something:

- better;
- worse;
- safer;
- harmful;
- useful;
- efficient;
- preferable;
- aligned;
- improved;

the evaluator, criterion, comparison basis, and relevant conditions should be represented where practical.

This does not prohibit evaluative language.

It exposes the relation producing the evaluation.

When a contribution assigns inquiry priority or resources, the allocation basis, allocator, resource conditions, and relevant dependencies should likewise remain represented where practical.

A decision to investigate one unresolved branch rather than another can therefore preserve the relevant criteria, values, allocation basis, and resource conditions without converting that decision into a claim about truth.

No neutral allocator is assumed.

An allocator can use expected information gain, urgency, cost, tractability, novelty, anomaly density, external request, random sampling, safety constraints, available instruments, compute, memory, or other represented bases.

Those bases can guide finite attention without becoming epistemic support for the proposition receiving attention.

Code changes should preserve the semantic distinctions claimed by the framework or explicitly document where the implementation does not yet represent them.

Particular attention should be given to:

```text
not_allocated != rejected

```

Implementation changes involving generation should test whether candidate and inquiry-operation generation remain distinguishable from allocation and evaluation.

Implementation changes involving inquiry basis should test whether an inquiry basis can remain represented without automatically producing priority, allocation, activation, or epistemic support.

Implementation changes involving priority should test whether priority remains distinguishable from actual resource allocation.

Implementation changes involving allocation should test whether resource allocation remains distinguishable from epistemic support and whether an unallocated inquiry remains distinguishable from a rejected inquiry.

Implementation changes involving activation should test whether open inquiry remains distinguishable from active inquiry.

Implementation changes involving allocator genealogy should test whether changes in allocation basis, priority, resources, activation, and deactivation remain reconstructable.

Implementation changes involving genealogy should test whether later recontextualization can add relations without erasing earlier states.

Implementation changes involving unresolved branches should test whether inactive or unallocated branches remain available for later recontextualization and reopening.

Implementation changes involving comparison should test whether absence of a represented ranking basis remains distinguishable from intrinsic incommensurability.

Tests should attack these distinctions rather than merely confirm expected outputs.

A passing implementation test does not establish the conceptual framework as true.

A failing test does not by itself establish the conceptual framework as false.

The relation between specification, implementation, test, and result remains represented.

## Recursive application

These contribution rules are themselves subject to contribution.

A contributor can challenge:

- the required fields;
- the definition of contribution;
- the distinction between basis and support;
- the distinction between acceptance basis and support;
- the distinction between inquiry basis and support;
- the distinction between inquiry basis and inquiry priority;
- the distinction between inquiry priority and resource allocation;
- the distinction between allocation and support;
- the distinction between support and measurement;
- the distinction between distinction and measurement;
- the distinction between generation and allocation;
- the distinction between allocation and evaluation;
- the distinction between generation and evaluation;
- the distinction between open and active inquiry;
- the comparison architecture;
- the allocation architecture;
- the treatment of alternatives;
- the branch-preservation protocol;
- the recontextualization model;
- the semantic invariants;
- the implementation requirements;
- the contribution process itself.

The contribution protocol must not become a protected layer that Aperta Veritas refuses to examine.

Its own rules can impose generative boundaries by determining which contributions are easy or difficult to express.

Its own requirements can impose allocation boundaries by affecting which contributions receive attention, testing, implementation effort, or other resources.

Those boundaries also remain available for examination.

## Minimal principle

A contribution does not need to agree with Aperta Veritas.

It needs enough represented structure for its relation to the current framework to be examined without silently converting disagreement, acceptance, inquiry basis, inquiry priority, resource allocation, measurement, confidence, consensus, value, reward, or selection into truth or support, and without assuming that the currently represented or currently active possibility space exhausts what can be discovered.
