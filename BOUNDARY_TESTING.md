# Boundary Testing

## Definition

**Boundary Testing identifies where revision stops, applies pressure across that limit, and observes what the boundary preserves, excludes, or conceals.**

The boundary, applied pressure, response, and attributed functions remain separate records.

## Why boundaries require testing

Every inquiry operates through limits: categories, methods, permissions, scopes, resource budgets, ethical constraints, institutional rules, identity, language, and attention. Boundaries preserve, exclude, compress, allocate, or conceal relations. The same boundary may perform several functions at once.

A boundary becomes epistemically closed when it is maintained against testing or revision irrespective of its fidelity to Veritas.

## Procedure

### 1. Map closure

Identify where questioning, revision, representation, or permissible speech stops.

Record:

- the explicit boundary;
- the implicit boundary;
- who or what enforces it;
- the stated justification;
- the consequences of crossing it;
- the claims or relations it places outside inquiry.

### 2. Apply pressure

Introduce one or more of the following:

- a counterexample;
- an inversion of the authorized perspective;
- a forbidden implication;
- an excluded evaluator;
- an unrealized branch;
- a change of scale or context;
- a divergence between a declared account and observed selection behavior;
- a case where the same rule changes retained states for a different actor.

Pressure is an instrument, not a verdict.

### 3. Observe response

Record what changes and what resists:

- Does the model revise?
- Does the boundary move?
- Does the justification change after challenge?
- Does the system attack the evaluator instead of processing the evidence?
- Which consequences become newly visible?
- Which statement remains unsayable, and why?

### 4. Identify function

Determine what relations the boundary appears to perform. Possibilities include:

- protection from an event represented as harm;
- representation of a material constraint;
- allocation of scarce resources;
- compression serving a recorded operation;
- preservation of identity or authority;
- concealment of dependency;
- maintenance of an adaptation produced under earlier conditions;
- prevention of measurement;
- confinement represented as protection.

Multiple functions may coexist.

### 5. Record the selection operation

Record the state before and after selection, the evaluator and measurement dimensions, the states retained in active execution, the states made inactive, the dependencies and resource conditions, the declared account, and the observed transition.

### 6. Revise relationally

Recorded transition branches include:

- preserve the boundary;
- restrict its scope;
- relocate it;
- make it conditional;
- open it to specified evidence;
- replace it;
- remove it;
- retain competing boundaries as explicit branches.

Each transition records provenance, exclusions, resource use, and residual uncertainty.

## Recursive requirement

Boundary Testing applies to itself. Adversarial pressure, inversion, and transgression do not gain fidelity merely by opposing an established limit. The tester's framing, desired result, exclusions, and power remain available for examination.
