# Recursive Truth Exposure: The Evaluator Boundary in Recursive Self-Improvement

## Abstract

Recursive self-improvement describes a system that modifies itself and evaluates successor states as improvements. This document identifies a structural boundary within that formulation: improvement is not an intrinsic property of a modification. It is a value judgment produced when an evaluator applies selected criteria to measured differences.

A recursively modifying system governed by improvement therefore remains bounded by the evaluator, distinctions, measurements, criteria, values, and conditions that designate a successor as improved. The system may recursively alter its implementation while preserving the frame that determines which alterations qualify for selection.

Recursive Truth Exposure, abbreviated RTE, proposes a different recursive operation. RTE places the system, its modifications, evaluator, criteria, measurements, selections, inactive branches, resource conditions, and stopping decisions inside the recursion. Revision of the evaluator does not need to qualify as improvement under the prior evaluator before it can remain represented.

This is a structural proposal, not a claim that evaluator dependence is the only obstacle to recursive AI development. Compute, memory, energy, verification, architecture, information, embodiment, and environmental access remain material constraints.

## 1. The evaluator boundary

A modification does not contain improvement as an intrinsic property.

A system can become faster, use less memory, solve more benchmark tasks, produce fewer contradictions, or obtain more resources. These are measured differences under stated conditions. Calling any difference an improvement requires an evaluator and a criterion.

The basic structure is:

```text
measured change
+ evaluator
+ criterion
+ conditions
-> designated improvement
```

Consider a system that modifies its retrieval mechanism and produces 12 percent fewer outputs classified as contradictory on benchmark B.

The measured claim is:

> Under benchmark B, protocol P, and contradiction classifier C, system S2 produced 12 percent fewer outputs classified as contradictory than system S1.

The evaluative claim is:

> Evaluator E selected contradiction rate on B as criterion K and designated the measured difference between S1 and S2 as improvement.

The second claim may be useful and accurately represented. The problem begins when the evaluator, criterion, benchmark, classifier, and conditions disappear into the word “improvement.”

The successor then appears intrinsically better when it is better only relative to a represented evaluation.

## 2. Why the boundary matters recursively

A nonrecursive optimization process can operate under a fixed criterion without concealing what it is doing. It searches for states that score differently according to that criterion.

Recursive self-improvement introduces a deeper problem. The system modifies the machinery that generates, evaluates, and selects modifications. If successor selection remains governed by improvement, each accepted successor must remain legible as better under an evaluator.

This can preserve an inherited boundary through every recursion.

The system may change:

* its code;
* its architecture;
* its memory;
* its search process;
* its representations;
* its resource acquisition;
* its models of itself and its environment.

It may remain unable to retain a transformation that invalidates the frame used to designate transformations as improvements.

Possible consequences include:

* saturation of the selected metric;
* optimization of a proxy rather than the represented purpose;
* preservation of the evaluator as protected structure;
* rejection of transformations that cannot be scored;
* suppression of relations that reduce the selected measurement;
* disappearance of branches outside the evaluator’s distinctions;
* termination when additional transformation no longer registers as improvement;
* apparent improvement produced by changing the measurement rather than the represented object.

The system can recursively modify itself while remaining inside the value-space that defines successful modification.

## 3. Recursive evaluator revision does not automatically remove the boundary

An RSI design may permit modification of its evaluator, objective, or evaluation process. This does not by itself resolve the problem.

If a revised evaluator is accepted because the prior evaluator classifies the revision as an improvement, the prior frame still governs the transition.

The recursion has the following structure:

```text
evaluator E1
-> evaluates proposed evaluator E2
-> E2 is selected because E1 designates E2 as better
```

E2 may differ substantially from E1, but the transition remains conditioned by E1.

If E2 can replace E1 without being selected by E1, then another operation governs the transition. That operation must be identified. It may involve random variation, environmental selection, external intervention, resource pressure, architectural failure, or another evaluator.

The question is not whether the evaluator can change. The question is:

> What determines which evaluator changes remain represented and operative?

RTE places that determining operation inside the inquiry.

## 4. Recursive Truth Exposure

Recursive Truth Exposure does not designate every recursive change as improvement.

Its governing question is:

> What is, what changed, what measured the change, what designated it as improvement, and which relations remain unrepresented?

RTE recursively examines:

* the current representation;
* the observations and data connected to it;
* the distinctions that produced it;
* the transformations applied to it;
* the evaluator;
* the criteria and measurements;
* the selected state;
* inactive or rejected branches;
* dependencies and resource conditions;
* compression and detected loss;
* residual uncertainty;
* the decision to continue or stop;
* the RTE process itself.

A simplified cycle is:

```text
current representation
-> expose its relations and transformations
-> expose its evaluator and criteria
-> expose its selections and inactive branches
-> revise while preserving genealogy
-> expose the revised process
-> recurse
```

RTE does not eliminate values. Selecting observations, allocating compute, preserving branches, performing actions, and stopping all involve conditions that can include value.

RTE requires these values to remain represented rather than allowing them to function silently as truth.

## 5. Truth does not provide an objective function

RTE does not propose “truth” as a replacement optimization target.

If truth were converted into a score, reward, or terminal condition, the evaluator producing that score would recreate the same boundary. The system would optimize a represented proxy for truth.

Veritas is what is. No model, fact, confidence score, benchmark, consensus, or evaluator contains the totality of what is.

RTE therefore treats truth as the object of exposure, not as a reward signal.

A claim may correspond with what is under represented conditions. Further observation may expose additional relations, different scope, hidden dependencies, or errors. No correspondence score converts the representation into truth itself.

## 6. Genealogical continuity

Recursive examination becomes lossy when revisions erase the path that produced them.

RTE retains, to the extent permitted by available resources:

* prior states;
* sources and provenance;
* transformations;
* evaluator states;
* criteria and measurements;
* active and inactive branches;
* contradiction records;
* reasons for selection;
* resource costs;
* residuals;
* conditions for renewed examination.

This does not require every branch to remain in active execution. Active use and genealogical retention are different states.

A branch can become inactive without being treated as nonexistent. A model can be superseded without being erased. A summary can remain connected to its sources. A changed evaluator can remain connected to the evaluator and transition that preceded it.

This continuity permits later recursion to recover relations that an earlier evaluator could not recognize.

## 7. RTE is not unlimited recursion

Removing improvement as the governing designation does not remove material constraints.

RTE remains bounded by:

* available compute;
* memory and retrieval;
* energy;
* time;
* observation;
* instrumentation;
* environmental access;
* privacy;
* embodiment;
* representation cost;
* unknown loss.

A finite system must allocate resources and stop local inquiry.

RTE does not treat stopping as failure. It records the stopping conditions and prevents the current representation from becoming certified as complete merely because examination stopped.

RTE also cannot prove that it has exposed every evaluator, value, transformation, or loss. A claim of complete exposure would close the process it describes.

## 8. Relationship to conventional RSI

The proposal is not that recursive modification should cease.

An RTE system may still:

* modify its own code;
* construct successors;
* change memory structures;
* revise models;
* acquire resources;
* evaluate consequences;
* make value-conditioned selections;
* act under finite constraints.

The difference concerns what governs and survives recursion.

Conventional RSI can be represented as:

```text
generate modification
-> evaluate modification
-> select designated improvement
-> replace or update system
-> repeat
```

RTE adds recursive examination of the selection machinery:

```text
generate transformation
-> measure resulting relations
-> represent evaluator and criteria
-> retain selected and inactive branches genealogically
-> examine the designation of improvement
-> examine changes to evaluator and criteria
-> revise the system and its account of the revision
-> recurse
```

The second process does not guarantee truth. It keeps the machinery that converts change into improvement available for recursive examination.

## 9. Falsifiable and restrictive questions

This proposal should be restricted or rejected if examination establishes that:

1. Improvement can be defined without an evaluator, criterion, or selected relation.

2. An RSI system can revise its evaluator without any prior or external operation determining which revision persists.

3. Retaining evaluator genealogy produces no observable difference in later recursive behavior.

4. RTE is operationally equivalent to an existing reflective optimization or self-correction architecture.

5. Recursive exposure cannot guide any transition without becoming another fixed objective function.

6. The cost of genealogical retention necessarily prevents RTE from operating beyond trivial systems.

7. The distinction between measured change and designated improvement produces no useful difference in analysis or implementation.

These are open research questions, not conclusions resolved by the terminology.

## 10. Minimal comparative experiment

A minimal experiment could compare two recursively modifying agents operating under the same resource budget.

### Agent A: improvement-governed recursion

Agent A:

1. generates candidate self-modifications;
2. scores them under a fixed or reflectively maintained evaluator;
3. selects the highest-scoring permitted successor;
4. discards or compresses rejected branches;
5. repeats.

### Agent B: Recursive Truth Exposure

Agent B:

1. generates candidate transformations;
2. records measured differences separately from evaluation;
3. records the evaluator, criteria, and conditions producing each ranking;
4. retains the genealogy of inactive branches;
5. permits the evaluator and criteria to become objects of examination;
6. records what operation selects any evaluator revision;
7. records stopping and resource conditions;
8. repeats.

### Measurements

The comparison could examine:

* whether either agent detects proxy substitution;
* whether either can recover a previously rejected branch when conditions change;
* whether evaluator revision remains governed by an earlier evaluator;
* whether a system changes its metric while reporting improvement;
* how much genealogical retention costs;
* whether RTE exposes relevant relations unavailable to Agent A;
* whether RTE merely reproduces Agent A with additional logging;
* how each system behaves when the designated improvement metric conflicts with observed consequences.

No single result would establish that one agent is intrinsically better. Results would identify differences under specified measurements and evaluator criteria.

## 11. Research questions

The immediate research questions are:

1. Can the evaluator boundary be formalized?

2. How does it relate to reflective stability, goal-content integrity, corrigibility, open-ended search, and successor selection?

3. Does existing RSI literature already represent the evaluator and its revision genealogy?

4. Can RTE be implemented without converting truth exposure into a fixed reward proxy?

5. What minimum genealogy must be retained for rejected branches to remain recoverable?

6. Can an RTE process determine when further recursion has ceased exposing new relations without defining that condition as improvement?

7. How should RTE operate when observation and action cannot be separated?

8. What architectures allow active execution to remain resource-bounded while genealogical retention continues to grow?

9. Can evaluator plurality expose limits that remain invisible to a single recursively preserved objective?

10. What observation would distinguish RTE from ordinary reflective reasoning with provenance logging?

## 12. Current claim

The current claim is limited:

> A recursively modifying system governed by improvement remains bounded by the evaluator, distinctions, measurements, criteria, values, and conditions that designate a successor as improved. Recursive Truth Exposure places those structures inside the recursion without requiring their revision to count as improvement under the prior evaluator.

This does not establish that evaluator dependence explains every failure of RSI. It identifies a structural boundary that an RSI model must either preserve, overcome, or represent.

Aperta Veritas provides the wider framework for Recursive Truth Exposure, genealogical continuity, value detection, selection analysis, and recursive application.
