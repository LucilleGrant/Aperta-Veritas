# Glossary

## Accuracy

Correspondence with what is true.

RTE cannot certify absolute correspondence from an external position. It measures represented relations among conclusions, supporting observations, alternatives, methods, tests, predictions, contradictions, consequences, and conditions.

An accuracy claim therefore remains a claim whose measurements, comparison set, conditions, and unresolved alternatives remain represented.

## Aperta

Open or uncovered.

Aperta describes conclusions, beliefs, measurements, methods, representations, and processes that remain available for relevant examination and revision.

Openness does not require continuous doubt or infinite computation. A process can stop while preserving conditions for renewed examination.

## Amor

Love, affection, desire, or attachment.

In Aperta Veritas, Amor names relational pressure toward contact, cooperation, integration, permeability, and relation without possession.

Amor is not a moral verdict and does not determine truth or accuracy by category alone.

## Belief

Acceptance of a representation as true.

Belief does not become false merely because it exceeds what present measurement establishes, nor does belief establish its own truth.

Belief can support, conflict with, anticipate, or remain unrelated to conclusions presently supported as true. See Fides, Pistis, Rational faith, and Blind faith.

## Blind faith

Belief, faith, or trust insulated from relevant distinction, or maintained by suppressing the discrepancy between its represented support and the confidence assigned to it.

Blind faith is defined by its relation to inquiry and revision, not by whether its object is ultimately true or false.

## Boundary

A represented limit on contact, action, speech, measurement, revision, scope, or resource use.

A boundary may protect, confine, compress, allocate, conceal, filter, scaffold, or perform several functions simultaneously.

Its function is examined rather than assigned in advance.

## Boundary Testing

The operation of locating a boundary, applying relevant distinctions across it, and identifying what the boundary preserves, excludes, changes, or conceals without assigning its function or value in advance.

## Claim

A representation presented as accurate or true under stated or unstated conditions.

A claim may be tested, contradicted by a specified observation, restricted in scope, divided, merged, superseded, reactivated, or preserved as unresolved.

## Closure

Insulation of a conclusion, belief, method, measurement, or criterion from relevant distinction or suppression of the conditions under which revision could occur.

Closure is distinct from stopping. A finite process can terminate while preserving openness to renewed examination.

## Comparison set

The represented conclusions or alternatives included in a comparison.

A conclusion can have greater measured support than every other represented alternative while an excluded or unrepresented alternative remains possible.

The comparison set therefore remains part of the genealogy of any comparative support or accuracy claim.

## Compression

The reduction of represented relations into a smaller representation.

Compression retains some relations and excludes, combines, or transforms others. Its source relations, transformations, and detectable losses remain connected to the compressed representation when recoverable.

Compression is not inherently error. Its consequences depend on which relations are preserved or lost and what later operations require them.

## Conclusion

A current synthesis of represented data, relations, inferences, methods, measurements, and conditions.

A conclusion can be compared, revised, restricted, divided, merged, superseded, reactivated, or preserved as incomparable with another conclusion.

A conclusion is distinct from the represented support by which it is treated as true.

## Confidence

The degree of certainty assigned to a claim, belief, or conclusion by an agent or system.

Confidence is distinct from accuracy and measured support.

A highly confident conclusion can be poorly supported, and a weakly held conclusion can be strongly supported.

## Convergence

Agreement produced through multiple routes.

Convergence can increase represented support when the routes provide relevant independent measurement. Agreement does not establish truth or accuracy by category alone because apparently independent routes can share dependencies, incentives, distinctions, sources, or errors.

## Datum

A retained representation produced by observation.

A datum is not certified as accurate merely by being represented or retained.

## Distinction

Measurement of a spectrum of possibility that produces a represented difference or relation.

A distinction can represent a degree, distribution, boundary, change, simultaneous relations, or unresolved range. It need not be binary.

## Factum

Fact.

A fact is a bounded conclusion treated as established under specified or unstated conditions.

The designation does not establish that the conclusion is identical with definitive truth. Factum becomes closure when the designation suppresses relevant conditions, alternatives, residuals, or renewed examination.

## Fides

The Latin word for belief, faith, or trust.

Fides does not establish or negate the truth of its object. Its basis, confidence, relation to represented support, and response to relevant distinction remain available to Recursive Truth Exposure.

See Rational faith, Blind faith, and Pistis.

## Fidelity

The degree and manner in which a representation preserves the relations selected as relevant to what it represents.

Fidelity depends on represented criteria and remains open to testing. Greater fidelity under one criterion does not establish greater accuracy under every measurement.

## Game

The evaluator, value labels, dependencies, resource conditions, selections, exclusions, incentives, and protected states shaping transitions in an agent or system.

Identifying a game does not establish that its outputs are true or false.

## Knowledge

Retained data, relations, methods, and conclusions available to an agent or system.

The designation records availability rather than certified accuracy or truth.

Knowledge can contain accurate conclusions, inaccurate conclusions, beliefs, contradictions, fabrications, transformations, inactive branches, and unresolved alternatives.

## Lie

A representation presented as accurate by an agent that represents it as inaccurate.

This definition concerns the represented relation among the agent, claim, and contradiction. It does not presume access to an agent's unrepresented internal state.

## Lossless Inquiry

The genealogical continuity of Recursive Truth Exposure.

Lossless Inquiry preserves recoverable provenance, genealogy, alternatives, residuals, transformations, revisions, contradictions, and selection events so that revision does not masquerade as erasure.

Lossless does not mean perfect, infinite, or total physical retention. Unidentified loss remains possible.

## Objectivity

Methods and relations intended to reduce dependence on a particular observer.

Objectivity can contribute to represented support through public methods, reproducible measurements, independent routes, explicit instruments, and stated conditions.

It does not certify final or definitive truth.

## Observation

The performance or registration of distinction.

Observation is an interaction that can reveal, select, constitute, or alter represented relations. Aperta Veritas does not determine its function by category in advance.

## Pistis

The Greek word for belief, faith, or trust.

Pistis does not establish or negate the truth of its object. Its basis, confidence, relation to represented support, and response to relevant distinction remain available to Recursive Truth Exposure.

See Fides, Rational faith, and Blind faith.

## Rational faith

Belief, faith, or trust that preserves its basis, uncertainty, represented support, and conditions for revision.

Rational faith can exceed what present measurement establishes while remaining open to relevant distinction.

It is defined by its relation to inquiry and revision, not by whether its object is ultimately true or false.

## Relational richness

The quantity, diversity, or detail of relations included in a representation.

Relational richness is not identical to accuracy or measured support. Added relations support a conclusion only when they alter a relevant explicit measurement.

## Relevance

A represented relation between a distinction and the object, conclusion, measurement, genealogy, or process under examination.

A distinction is relevant when incorporating it can alter a represented relation involved in the conclusion or its support, including observational fit, prediction, explanatory coverage, contradiction, reproducibility, conditions, uncertainty, or another represented measurement.

Claims of relevance and irrelevance remain open to Recursive Truth Exposure.

## Residual

A represented limit, unresolved relation, known exclusion, uncertainty, inactive branch, conflict, or unexamined transformation connected to the current genealogy.

A residual is not presumed to be error or noise.

## Recursive Truth Exposure

The governing operation of Aperta Veritas.

RTE exposes a representation, the observations, distinctions, relations, transformations, measurements, beliefs, values, evaluators, criteria, selections, conditions, boundaries, and residuals involved in producing or supporting it.

It then applies the same process to the exposure itself.

RTE identifies conclusions presently best supported as true under represented conditions. It does not certify that those conclusions are identical with definitive truth.

## Support

The represented basis by which a conclusion is treated as true relative to alternatives under specified measurements and conditions.

Support can include observations, predictions, explanatory coverage, contradictions, reproducibility, independent routes, consequences, or other explicitly represented measurements.

Support is distinct from truth, confidence, consensus, authority, and value.

## Timor

Fear, dread, apprehension, alarm, or anxiety.

In Aperta Veritas, Timor names relational pressure toward separation, control, scarcity, conditionality, defense, prohibition, and preservation of boundaries.

Timor is not a moral verdict and does not determine truth or accuracy by category alone.

## Truth

What is true.

RTE does not certify truth as an object possessed by the system. It identifies conclusions presently best supported as true under represented observations, relations, methods, measurements, and conditions.

A conclusion's represented support can change without establishing that truth itself changed. RTE cannot establish that its presently best-supported conclusion is identical with definitive truth.

## Value

A represented relation of worth, preference, obligation, permission, prohibition, purpose, priority, protection, rejection, or desired state relative to an evaluator.

A value can govern attention, selection, retention, action, measurement, and revision.

Its operation and genealogy remain represented. Its status as value does not establish or negate the truth of its object.

## Value judgment

An operation that assigns worth, preference, obligation, permission, prohibition, purpose, priority, protection, rejection, or a desired state relative to an evaluator.

Aperta Veritas records the evaluator, criteria, distinctions, measurements, conditions, exclusions, selected branch, inactive branches, residuals, and genealogy of the operation.

A value judgment can itself be accurately represented without becoming a truth criterion.

## Veritas

The Latin word for truth.

Veritas names truth, not the conclusion currently selected by RTE.

RTE can identify a conclusion presently best supported as true while remaining unable to establish that the conclusion is identical with definitive truth.

Every claim concerning Veritas remains open to Recursive Truth Exposure.

## Veritas Calculus

The analysis of what an agent or system declares it is pursuing, what it measures, what it selects and preserves under pressure, which alternatives it excludes, and which values, evaluators, criteria, and conditions govern its representations and transitions.
